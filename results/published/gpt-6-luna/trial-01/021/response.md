# C412 overdue receivable: EBITDA, working capital and price

## Conclusion

Treat C412 as a **specific collection and purchase-price risk**, not as an EBITDA add-back. At 31 December 2025, Riverbend Equipment LLC (customer C412) had **$1.800 million more than 90 days past due** and no booked allowance. Subsequent records show **$600,000 collected against that old balance**, but as of 12 February 2026 Riverbend could not commit to a date for the remaining **$1.200 million** and said refinancing discussions were continuing. I would protect the buyer for the remaining $1.2 million—preferably by excluding it from eligible closing NWC and/or a dollar-for-dollar holdback/escrow until collected—rather than treating the face amount as ordinary, collectible working capital. Do not apply both a full NWC exclusion and a second price deduction for the same $1.2 million.

Separately, a **$300,000 correction to a December 2025 C412 invoice** is supported by a signed pre-year-end order and was posted by credit note in January. It corrects the agreed 2025 selling price, not the overdue-balance risk. Reduce 2025 sales and EBITDA by $300,000 (assuming no associated cost reduction; goods and quantities were unchanged) and correct year-end AR by the same amount. Do not add the correction back in adjusted EBITDA.

Thus C412's 31 December AR shown in the ageing totals **$4.967m** ($1.800m overdue plus $3.167m December invoices). After correcting the pre-existing $300,000 price overstatement, the comparable AR is **$4.667m**. Subsequent receipts of $3.467m ($2.867m on December invoices plus $0.600m on summer invoices) leave **$1.200m** of the identified December-end C412 exposure unresolved. This is a reconciliation of the identified C412 invoices, not a claim that the customer's entire account has no other activity after year-end.

C412 is Riverbend Equipment LLC, not one of the Kestrel-controlled customer entities: the customer master identifies C412 as Riverbend, and its 30 January ownership declaration says it is unrelated to Kestrel or Meridian. The risk is therefore an independent customer's credit risk, not an identified intercompany balance.

## Recommended deal treatment

### Adjusted EBITDA

- **Do not deduct the $1.200m face value directly from EBITDA just because it is overdue.** A/R is a balance-sheet asset; delayed collection by itself is not an EBITDA expense.
- The 2025 accounts show **no allowance for credit losses and no credit-loss expense** (Trial Balance, December rows 503 and 541). That is not evidence that the $1.2m is collectible. The long delinquency, partial rather than full cure, and Riverbend's inability to commit to payment support a specific collectability assessment.
- Require management's documented expected-recovery estimate and the underlying evidence. If an expected credit-loss provision/write-off is required for the 2025 financials, reflect the estimated loss as an operating credit-loss cost in EBITDA; **do not add it back as “non-recurring”** absent evidence that such credit losses are genuinely exceptional. The records do not establish that the entire $1.2m is irrecoverable, so $1.2m is a conservative underwriting exposure/price-protection amount, not a proven accounting loss.
- Independently, correct 2025 revenue and EBITDA downward by **$300,000** for the December invoice billed above the signed price. The January credit note documents an error in the year-end invoice; it does not document a new January concession or a credit-loss event.

### Working-capital peg / closing NWC

Keep the normal operating NWC peg methodology, but do **not** give the seller full face-value NWC credit for the $1.2m aged balance without collection support. Either:

1. define this specific >90-day C412 receivable as ineligible/excluded from closing NWC (and apply the definition consistently to the peg and closing calculation); or
2. use a specific dollar-for-dollar escrow/holdback or true-up for the $1.2m, released to the seller only as cash is collected or recoverability is otherwise demonstrated.

The $300,000 price correction is a separate year-end AR correction: it reduces both 2025 sales and the relevant 31 December receivable. Ensure the SPA's EBITDA valuation and NWC mechanics do not inadvertently value the incorrect invoice or count the same credit-loss exposure twice.

### Price

For conservative pricing, protect against **$1.2m** until collected or secured. A direct price deduction or escrow is preferable to a broad multiple haircut to EBITDA for the receivable principal. If the buyer instead excludes the receivable from NWC, do not also deduct the same $1.2m again as a separate price adjustment. The separate **$300,000** year-end price correction should be reflected in the EBITDA/financial-statement bridge and AR/NWC schedule, with SPA mechanics checked to avoid duplicate recovery for the same billing error.

## Basis and reasoning

- The 31 December 2025 receivables ageing identifies invoices **I202506000401, I202507000401 and I202508000401** at $600,000 open each, aged 179, 149 and 118 days respectively. The same sheet shows four December invoices totaling $3,166,666.66 as current and a booked allowance of zero (Receivables_2025_12.xlsx, sheet “Receivables 2025-12-31,” rows 41–47).
- The settlements history shows only $191,666.67 originally received on each summer invoice, leaving $600,000 each (Customer_settlements.xlsx, sheet “Receipts,” rows 875, 923 and 962). It then records $200,000 paid to each invoice on 26 January 2026 (rows 1162–1164), confirming $1.2m still open after those payments. Riverbend's 12 February remittance email says $600,000 was transferred against the three summer invoices, that it could not commit to a date for the remaining $1.2m while refinancing discussions continued, and that no further remittance advice was available as of that date.
- The December-order receivables were subsequently settled: the settlements sheet records the first two invoices on 9 and 16 January, the corrected third invoice on 23 January, and the fourth on 30 January (rows 1145, 1148, 1153, 1159 and 1192). The $300,000 credit is recorded against invoice I202512000403 on 12 January (row 1148).
- The commercial evidence says the December shipment's accepted price was **$494,166.66** under Riverbend_PO_251219.pdf, page 1, which superseded the prior quotation. CN_260112_01.pdf, page 1, says invoice I202512000403 used the superseded price sheet, that the signed order and acceptance fixed the lower price before year-end, and that goods and quantities were unchanged. The credit note is also in Sales_register_2026-01.xlsx, sheet “Sales,” on C412's CN-260112-01 line. Accordingly this is a 2025 transaction-price correction, despite the January posting date.
- C412's 2025 sales register records $38.000m net sales and $24.320m product cost (Sales_register_2025.xlsx, sheet “Sales,” C412 rows 293–388, calculated from the underlying invoice and credit lines). The December C412 lines are rows 381–388. Applying the documented $300,000 correction reduces net sales to $37.700m and, with unchanged product cost, gross profit/EBITDA by $300,000. Management's Trading_update.docx, table 1, reports C412 December net sales of $3,166,666.66 and does not reflect the subsequent correction.
- The December 2025 trial balance shows $27.300m trade receivables, no credit-loss allowance and no credit-loss expense (Trial_balance_2025.xlsx, sheet “Trial Balance,” rows 502–503 and 541). The absence of a reserve is inconsistent with treating the overdue amount as automatically good, but the data room does not provide enough evidence to quantify the expected recovery precisely.
- Customer_master.xlsx, sheet “Customers,” identifies C412 as Riverbend Equipment LLC. Ownership_C412.pdf, page 1, declares no common ownership with Kestrel or Meridian. This supports treating it as third-party credit exposure; it does not establish Riverbend's ability to pay.

## Limitations and follow-up

The evidence supports a **$1.2m unresolved exposure**, not a conclusion that the full amount is legally or economically unrecoverable. I did not find a committed repayment schedule, collateral/guarantee, Riverbend financial statements, refinancing outcome, insolvency filing, or later cash evidence beyond the 12 February email. Request updated bank receipts and remittance advice, a signed repayment plan, current Riverbend financials and refinancing status, any security/guarantees and lien priority, and management's invoice-level expected-credit-loss analysis. Update the closing NWC and escrow/price protection for any later cash collected. The no-addback conclusion assumes credit losses on trade receivables are part of ordinary operating risk; any contrary treatment would need specific evidence and consistent precedent.
