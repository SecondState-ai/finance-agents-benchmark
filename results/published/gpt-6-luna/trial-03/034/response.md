# FY2025 bonus: adjusted EBITDA and net-debt bridge

## Conclusion

**Do not add back the ordinary FY2025 bonus expense.** The books include **$600,000** of FY2025 bonus expense and a matching **$600,000 bonus payable** at 31 December 2025. It is a recurring accrual, consistent with the prior-year pattern, and should remain in EBITDA.

Separately, the board-approved FY2025 retention pool is **$1,200,000**, guaranteed to employees in service at 31 December and payable on **13 March 2026**. It is not conditional on a sale. The available year-end accounts show no separate expense or liability for this pool. On the evidence provided, it is an additional FY2025 compensation cost: **reduce reported FY2025 EBITDA by $1,200,000** and recognize the corresponding payable. It is not a defensible EBITDA add-back.

- Reported FY2025 EBITDA: **$21.466m**.
- EBITDA after correcting for the unrecorded pool: **$20.266m**, before any other adjustments.
- If all four adjustments in management's Earnings Schedule were accepted, its proposed $23.796m adjusted EBITDA would instead be **$22.596m** after the $1.200m bonus correction. That is an arithmetic sensitivity, not endorsement of the other adjustments.
- In the net-debt bridge, show the **unpaid $1.200m retention commitment** as a debt-like item (or otherwise deduct it under the agreed purchase-price mechanics), because it is a committed post-year-end cash payment omitted from the balance sheet. Do not also count it as a separate working-capital shortfall. Separately identify the booked **$600,000 ordinary bonus payable** and agree whether it is ordinary-course working capital or debt-like under the SPA; do not count it twice.

This treatment assumes the $1.200m pool is **in addition to**, not included within, the $600,000 ordinary bonus accrual. The records strongly suggest that distinction, but management should confirm it and provide the individual pool calculation before finalizing the bridge.

## Analysis and calculations

### 1. Ordinary bonus recorded in the books: $600,000

The 2025 payroll summary reports **$50,000 bonus expense per month**, January–December, totaling **$600,000**; bonus payable rises to **$600,000** by December. The 2025 Trial Balance records the same pattern: monthly debits to account **600200, Bonuses**, and credits to **210100, Bonus payable**. The December management-accounts balance sheet also shows a $600,000 credit in bonus payable. Thus this $600,000 is already an expense in reported EBITDA and is not an EBITDA add-back.

This looks like an ordinary recurring accrual, not the board's separate retention pool. In 2024, the same ledger accounts accrued $60,000 monthly ($720,000 for the year), and the books record payment of the prior-period $720,000 payable in 2025. A bonus paid after year-end is therefore consistent with the company's historical process, rather than evidence that the expense is exceptional.

### 2. Separate retention commitment: $1,200,000 not recorded

The 15 January 2025 board minutes and the **Retention pool memorandum** each state that the board guarantees a **$1,200,000 annual retention pool** for employees in service at 31 December 2025, approved on 15 January 2025, payable **13 March 2026**, and not conditional on a company sale. The condition described—service through 31 December—was met by the year-end population, according to the stated terms. This is evidence of a FY2025 compensation obligation, not a discretionary deal bonus.

However, the reported payroll summary, 2025 Trial Balance, and December management accounts show only $600,000 of bonus expense/payable and no separate $1.2m retention-pool entry. The December balance sheet instead has **$600,000** in bonus payable. The $1.2m pool should therefore be raised as a year-end completeness adjustment unless management demonstrates it is already captured elsewhere. On the stated separate commitment, the incremental expense is $1.2m; corrected EBITDA is $21.466m − $1.200m = **$20.266m**.

The management Earnings Schedule proposes $900,000 ERP, $480,000 severance, $300,000 salary and $650,000 legal-settlement add-backs, totaling $2.330m. Its implied adjusted EBITDA is $21.466m + $2.330m = $23.796m. Including the missing retention expense gives $23.796m − $1.200m = **$22.596m**, assuming all those other adjustments are accepted. This calculation does not assess their separate merits.

### 3. Net-debt bridge treatment

The retention pool has a stated payment date after the balance-sheet date and no corresponding liability appears in the reported 31 December balance sheet. If unpaid at the transaction measurement date, include the **$1.2m outstanding amount** as debt-like in the bridge, subject to the SPA's definitions and any proof of payment. Alternatively, record it as a liability and reflect it through the agreed net-debt / working-capital mechanics—but not in both places.

The existing $600,000 bonus payable is different: it has already been accrued through FY2025 EBITDA. Determine from the SPA whether that ordinary-course accrued payroll item remains in working capital or is treated as debt-like. Whichever classification is agreed, use it once. Do not treat the same booked $600,000 as both a working-capital deduction and a debt-like deduction. Also do not treat the $1.2m pool as already accrued merely because the balance sheet has a $600,000 bonus-payable balance: the payroll and ledger detail supports a separate monthly accrual.
