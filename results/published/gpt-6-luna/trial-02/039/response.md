# Do the cited efficiencies explain the margin improvement?

## Conclusion

**No—not on the evidence reviewed.** Reported gross margin increased from **36.0% in FY2024 to 38.0% in FY2025** (gross profit of **$43.20m** to **$54.72m**). But the full 2.0 percentage-point increase is explained by a **one-time $2.88m Atlas supplier transition allowance**, not by demonstrated sustainable pricing or fulfilment efficiency. The allowance was conditional on 2025 purchases, became unconditional only at 31 December 2025, applies to 2025 sold units, and is expressly **not renewable or available in 2026**.

Also, a **$300,000 Riverbend price correction** issued in January 2026 relates to a price already fixed in a signed December 2025 order. Treating it as a 2025 revenue correction, as the underlying facts indicate, reduces FY2025 gross margin to about **37.87% including the one-off allowance**, or **35.87% excluding the allowance**. On that basis, there is no remaining gross-margin improvement versus 2024.

## Margin calculation and bridge

| USD millions, except margins | FY2024 | FY2025 reported | FY2025 with Riverbend correction | FY2025 corrected, excluding one-off Atlas allowance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 120.00 | 144.00 | 143.70 | 143.70 |
| Cost of sales, net of rebates | 76.80 | 89.28 | 89.28 | 92.16 |
| Gross profit | 43.20 | 54.72 | 54.42 | 51.54 |
| Gross margin | 36.00% | 38.00% | 37.87% | 35.87% |

The reported increase in gross profit was **$11.52m**. The $2.88m Atlas allowance is exactly **2.0% of reported FY2025 revenue**; without it, FY2025 gross profit is $51.84m and margin is **36.0%**—the same as FY2024. The subsequent Riverbend correction lowers revenue and gross profit by a further $0.30m, taking the normalized margin below FY2024.

This is consistent with the routine sales pattern in the registers: monthly net sales/product cost were **$10.0m/$6.4m in FY2024** and generally **$11.5m/$7.36m in FY2025**, a 36% gross margin in both periods before the rebate. The extra December business does not appear to carry a superior margin: the signed Kestrel order and acceptance are for $6.0m, and the sales register has a December 29 $6.0m transaction with $3.84m product cost (36% gross margin). The register transaction is consistent in amount and timing with Kestrel, although its invoice reference does not itself identify the PO.

## Why the efficiency explanation is not supported

- **The allowance, not lower unit purchase cost, drove the margin expansion.** FY2025 purchase-register rows show $37.824m of Atlas/V100 purchases, above the $35m threshold, and a separate $2.88m allowance entry. The listed product unit prices remain $10 in both the 2024 and 2025 purchase registers. The Atlas letter says the allowance applies to sold 2025 units and will not recur. Other supplier terms reviewed also state fixed prices and no retrospective rebates.
- **The sales pattern does not show an improved underlying gross margin.** Sales registers and the closed-year trial balances support 36% gross margin before the Atlas allowance. Higher revenue and gross profit dollars therefore reflect more sales at roughly the same underlying margin, not an evidenced uplift in margin from fulfilment efficiencies.
- **Operating costs do not demonstrate broad fulfilment savings.** Board minutes report FY2025 payroll of $24.12m versus a $23.40m budget (and $21.816m in FY2024); outbound freight of $2.64m versus a $2.40m budget (and $2.40m in FY2024); and utilities, IT, insurance, selling and professional costs also above budget. Freight did grow more slowly than revenue, which may indicate some operating-cost leverage, but freight is below gross profit in the accounts and does not explain the gross-margin rate increase. No quantified labour productivity, handling-cost-per-order, pick/ship, waste, or comparable fulfilment KPI bridge was provided.
- **Sustainable pricing is not demonstrated as the cause of a higher underlying margin.** The recurring FY2025 monthly sales/cost pattern stays at the same 36% margin. Atlas prices are fixed through 30 June 2026; the proposed 4% renewal increase is pending acceptance, and the 2025 allowance will not recur.

## Riverbend adjustment and period-specific caveat

Riverbend’s signed 19 December 2025 order sets the shipment price at **$494,166.66**, superseding the prior quotation. The 2025 sales register records invoice **I202512000403** at **$794,166.66** (register row 385). Credit note **CN-260112-01** subsequently reduced the invoice by **$300,000**, explicitly because the December invoice used a superseded price sheet and the signed order had fixed the lower amount before year-end. This is evidence of a 2025 price/billing error, rather than a new 2026 concession. I have therefore shown a $300,000 2025 revenue and gross-profit correction in the normalized analysis.

This differs from Harbor’s separate $50,000 January goodwill credit: the credit note and correspondence say Harbor’s goods were accepted at the agreed price without defects and the disruption occurred after New Year. I have not treated that later goodwill concession as a 2025 adjustment.

## Limitations and follow-up

The management accounts are unaudited. The underlying registers support the reported totals, but the data room does not provide item-level customer pricing/volume analysis or operational productivity measures sufficient to independently quantify a pricing-versus-mix or fulfilment-efficiency bridge. The Riverbend adjustment is based on the order and credit note provided; confirm its posting and FY2025 treatment in the final close. I would request management’s transaction-level gross-margin bridge by SKU/customer, evidence of any claimed operational savings (including comparable units/orders, labour hours, freight per shipment and service levels), and confirmation that no other post-year-end credits or supplier allowances relate to FY2025.
